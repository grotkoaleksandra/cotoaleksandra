    Poker in 10 minutes

    The basics of Texas Hold'em, the most popular kind of poker. Read this once, then play with the helper switched on. It explains each moment as it happens.

    The goal

    Win chips. You win a hand in one of two ways:

    • you have the best five-card hand when the cards are shown at the end, or
    • every other player folds (gives up) before that.

    The second way is why poker isn't just luck. You can win with a weak hand if everyone else folds. Betting with a weak hand to make others fold is called a bluff.

    How one hand plays out

    1. The blinds. The two players to the left of the dealer button (D) must put in small forced bets: the small blind (10) and the big blind (20). That way there's always something to win. The button moves one seat to the left every hand.
    2. Hole cards. Everyone gets 2 private cards, face down.
    3. Pre-flop betting. Starting left of the big blind, each player folds, calls (pays 20 to stay in) or raises.
    4. The flop. 3 shared cards are dealt face up in the middle. Another betting round.
    5. The turn. A 4th shared card. Another betting round.
    6. The river. The 5th and last shared card. The last betting round.
    7. Showdown. Everyone still in shows their cards. The best five-card hand wins the pot.
    Your hand is the best 5 cards out of 7: your 2 private cards plus the 5 shared ones. You can use both of yours, just one, or even none.

    Hand rankings

    From best to worst. A hand higher on the list always beats one lower down.

    Ties: if two players have the same kind of hand, the higher cards win. A pair of Kings beats a pair of 7s. If the pairs are equal, the next highest card (the kicker) decides. Suits are all equal: hearts never beat spades.

    Position: why the dealer seat is the best

    After the flop, the player just left of the button acts first and the button acts last. Acting last is a big advantage: you see what everyone else did before you decide. Play more hands when you're on or near the button, and fewer when you have to act early.

    Which starting hands to play

    Most of poker happens before the flop: knowing which two cards are worth playing. A good beginner rule: play few hands, but when you play, bet and raise rather than just calling.

    GroupHandsWhat to do
    PremiumAA, KK, QQ, JJ, 10-10, A-KRaise. Happy to put lots of chips in.
    Strong99, 88, 77, A-Q, A-J suited, A-10 suited, K-Q suitedRaise or call a raise.
    PlayableSmall pairs (22–66), any A + suited card, two cards 10 or higher (K-Q, K-J, Q-J, J-10…), suited connectors like 8-7 or 6-5 of one suitPlay when it's cheap or you're near the button. Fold to big raises.
    FoldEverything else, like K-4, Q-7, 9-2Fold, unless you're in the big blind and can check for free.

    "Suited" means both cards are the same suit. With only one opponent you can play more hands than this, because one other player is less likely to have something good.

    Simple poker math

    Outs are the cards left in the deck that would improve your hand to a likely winner.

    • Flush draw (4 cards of one suit): 9 outs. There are 13 of each suit and you can see 4.
    • Open-ended straight draw (like 5-6-7-8, where a 4 or a 9 completes it): 8 outs.
    • Gutshot (like 5-6-_-8-9, only a 7 works): 4 outs.

    The rule of 2 and 4. Multiply your outs by 4 on the flop (two cards still to come) or by 2 on the turn (one card to come). That gives your rough chance in %. A flush draw on the flop: 9 × 4 ≈ 36%.

    Pot odds tell you if a call is worth it. Compare what you pay with what you could win. Say there are 80 chips in the pot (including the opponent's bet) and calling costs 20. After you call, the pot holds 100 and your 20 is 20% of it. So you need to win at least 20% of the time for the call to pay off in the long run. Win more often than that: call. Less: fold.

    The helper in the game does this maths for you and draws it as a bar: your chance of winning against a red line showing what you need.

    Beginner tips

    • Fold most hands before the flop. Good players fold around 3 out of 4 hands. Folding is not losing.
    • Bet your strong hands. If you just check a great hand, the others get to see more cards for free and might catch up.
    • Don't chase draws at any price. Use the pot odds. A small bet is worth calling with a draw, a huge one usually isn't.
    • Read the board. Three cards of one suit in the middle means someone could have a flush. Four cards in a row means a straight is possible. Your pair might not be good any more.
    • Raises usually mean strength, especially from careful players who rarely bet.
    • Bluff rarely, and against one opponent. Bluffing three people at once almost never works, because one of them usually has something.
    • Some players call everything. Don't bluff them. Bet your good hands against them and let them pay you.

    Words you'll hear

    Board
    The shared cards in the middle.
    Button / dealer
    The D marker. The best seat: it acts last after the flop.
    Blinds
    Forced bets (10 and 20 here) paid by the two players left of the button.
    Hole cards
    Your two private cards.
    Pot
    All the chips bet in this hand. The winner takes it.
    Kicker
    The highest card that isn't part of your pair (or other hand). It breaks ties.
    Top pair
    You paired the highest card on the board. Usually a good hand.
    Overpair
    Your pocket pair is higher than every card on the board.
    Set
    Three of a kind made with a pocket pair plus one card on the board. Very strong and well hidden.
    Draw
    A hand that isn't made yet but could be, like four cards to a flush.
    Outs
    Cards that would complete your draw.
    Heads-up
    Just two players in the hand.
    Stack
    How many chips a player has.
    The nuts
    The best possible hand with this board.
    Showdown
    When the remaining players show their cards at the end.
